# Canine Renal And Urinary Diagnostic Guidance

This document supports a synthetic diagnostic recommendation workspace for dogs.

## Common intake patterns
- Increased thirst with urinary accidents often supports a first-pass urinary workup.
- Straining to urinate, pollakiuria, or recurrent accidents increase the value of urine-focused diagnostics.
- Chronic kidney disease history can justify renal monitoring even when the presenting complaint is broad.

## Common next diagnostic actions
- `URINALYSIS` is a strong first-line action for lower urinary signs, accidents, or renal screening context.
- `URINE_CULTURE` is appropriate when infection or recurrent urinary inflammation is a meaningful concern.
- `SDMA_CHEM17` is appropriate when renal function, hydration, or chronic kidney monitoring is part of the workup.
- `UPC_RATIO` is generally a follow-on action when protein loss or renal monitoring is the question rather than an undifferentiated first pass.

## Relative preference notes
- Prefer `URINALYSIS` before `URINE_CULTURE` when the main question is initial urinary characterization.
- Prefer `SDMA_CHEM17` when increased thirst or renal history broadens the question beyond lower urinary signs.
- Reserve `UPC_RATIO` for cases where protein assessment or renal monitoring is explicitly relevant.

## Repeat-test cautions
- Avoid repeating the same urinary diagnostic inside its recent-test suppression window unless there is a documented change in severity or a new clinical question.
- When the same test was run recently, explain why it was suppressed instead of presenting it as the leading action.
